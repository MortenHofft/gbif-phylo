# Changelog

## Unreleased (2026-10-21: the library replaces the research on `main`; the research is on the `research` branch)

- **Summaries of collapsed clades, and preferring named clades in the overview** (#57, third part). The label of a collapsed clade is now "Name · 12,345 tips" (or "12,345 tips" without a name), through a new viewer option `formatCollapsedLabel(name, tips)` (default `defaultFormatCollapsedLabel`); the name follows `cladeLabel` and `formatLabel` as before. **Behaviour change:** a named collapsed clade's label also gives its size. New viewer helper `namedDescendants(tree, row, { formatLabel, cladeLabel }, { max, maxVisits })`: the highest named clades inside a clade (the shallowest internal nodes with a name by `cladeName`, breadth-first, not below a named one), stopping after `max` names or `maxVisits` children looked at (tips included), so it costs the same on a clade of a million tips. tree-ops `autoCollapse` and `mergeAutoCollapse` take `by: 'named'` (an experiment) with an optional `isNamed(node)`: unnamed clades open before named ones whatever their size, then the largest first, within the same budget. The explorer's "Overview: 50 clades" button is now "Collapse to 50 clades" (so "overview" means the overview control only), with a "Prefer named" toggle next to it; it hides Open Tree's `mrcaott…` names (`cladeLabel`); the hover box of a collapsed clade lists up to 5 named clades inside it and the Info panel up to 12, with the clade's tips. On GTDB 190k at 50 rows, 17 collapsed clades are named by size and 27 (all phyla) preferring names; on OTL 100k, 26 and 34 ([docs/design/auto-collapse-named.md](docs/design/auto-collapse-named.md)). Still open under #57: `viewer.autoCollapse(n)` sized from the pane, and whether to mark auto-collapsed clades.
- **Drill-down in the explorer: an overview of 50 clades, expand a collapsed clade in place or jump into it** (#57, second part). New tree-ops `mergeAutoCollapse(t, collapsed, { maxRows, root?, minTips? })`: the collapsed set with the clade `root` replaced by its own `autoCollapse` overview (`root` itself open, the ids outside it kept; one row per child when not even the children fit). The explorer has an "Overview: 50 clades" button for the clade shown; a click on a collapsed clade's triangle expands it in place with an overview of about 20 rows inside (the menu stays closed: it opens from a click on the clade's branch, or Enter), and Show only in a collapsed clade's menu jumps into it with an overview of 50 rows there, keeping it collapsed outside so Back returns to the view as it was. Checked in `check-explorer.mjs` on GTDB 190k: 50 rows, a click on the largest clade gives 69, Show only on the next gives 50, and Back the 69 again. Still to come under #57: `by: 'named'`, `viewer.autoCollapse(n)` sized from the pane, and the summaries of collapsed clades ("Name · N tips", the highest named descendants).
- **Radial, very large trees: every tip can be reached, and a pan does not fold tips back into a slice** (#76, #77). On trees of more than about a million tips the radial zoom stopped at 20,000 times the fit scale, with tips still closer than `minCladePx` (the explorer's 14) in a small pane, so polytomies of tips (Craterellus next to *Pterygellus polymorphus* on the 2.39M-tip Open Tree) never resolved; the ceiling now never stops before tips are 50 px apart (`RADIAL_MAX_TIP_PX`), so the zoom reaches `maxTipSpacing`. And a run of small siblings counted only the siblings in view, so panning a few more into view turned tips drawn one by one into a slice; now siblings outside the window count towards their run, in both layouts, and which tips are drawn depends on the zoom, not on where the window is. The cut goldens of 4 polytomy views are captured again. Cost: a zoomed cut on a large polytomy now costs about what the fit view does (a 200k-tip star: about 4 ms instead of 1 ms).
- **Explorer: a simpler node menu, styled as the inspector, and one popover radius** (#68). The explorer's node menu items are single lines with no descriptions (`hint` is no longer used); "Show only this clade" is now "Show only" and "Copy Newick of this clade" "Copy Newick", and *Swap with…* says in a toast to click a sibling branch. The menu shares the inspector's panel, header (with a line under it) and close button through the same CSS rules. Every explorer popover (toolbar menus, inspector, node menu, colour palette, search, hover box, toast) takes its corner rounding from one token, `--ex-popover-radius` (10px), and what sits inside one `calc(var(--ex-popover-radius) * 0.6)`. In the library, the `NodeMenu` header is now `.phylo-node-menu__head` holding the `.phylo-node-menu__title` and a close button (`.phylo-node-menu__close`, for the pointer, out of the tab order), with a line under it; keyboard focus and the arrows move over the items only. The popup radius is the existing `--phylo-radius`, the items' `--phylo-control-radius`; the explorer sets both from `--ex-popover-radius` on its pane.
- **Aligned labels in the radial layout** (#52). `alignLabels` now acts in a radial phylogram too: the tip labels start on one circle just outside the outermost tip, still along their rays, with dotted leaders along the rays from the tips (and collapsed clades) using the `leader*` tokens, the density rule, the cap and dimming of the rectangular leaders. Zoomed in so far that the circle is out of view, it is pinned at the furthest visible radius less room for the text. The radial fit keeps that room outside the circle (from the longest tip label, at most 20% of the smaller side), so with `alignLabels` on the fitted circle is smaller, and switching the option refits. Radial labels, aligned or not, are now hit areas (a click or hover on one is its tip), as rectangular labels were. The explorer's Align button works in the circular layout, and the playground's switch says so.
- **Collapse to an overview of the largest clades** (#57, first part). New tree-ops `autoCollapse(t, { maxRows, root?, minTips? })`: the node ids to pass to `viewer.setCollapsed` so that a clade (default the whole tree) shows at most `maxRows` rows, tips plus collapsed clades, opening the clades with the most tips first. A clade whose children don't fit (a wide polytomy) stays collapsed, so the budget always holds; `minTips` keeps small clades open; `root` works inside one clade, for drilling down. Iterative, with a max-heap of the collapsed clades: at `maxRows` 50 it takes under 0.1 ms on GTDB 190k, OTL Metazoa 1.49M and the full 2.39M OTL tree (Node 22, median of 9; 0.5 ms at `maxRows` 1000), and its unit test times it on a 2.4M-tip synthetic tree. The playground has a "Collapse to N clades" slider (0 = off), applied again when the shown clade changes (a shown root with more than N children gets one row per child), checked in `check-playground.mjs`. Still to come under #57: preferring named clades (`by: 'named'`), the drill-down actions and summaries of collapsed clades, and the explorer's overview.
- **Radial scale rings** (#45). New option `radialRings: 'none' | 'lines' | 'stripes'` (default `'none'`): circles about the root behind the radial tree (with branch lengths and `axis` on) at the scale bar's step, so the corner bar is always one band wide and zooming in subdivides them (the step drops to the next 1, 2 or 5 x 10^n) while zooming out merges them. On a dated tree they count back from the tips (age 0), otherwise out from the root; `'stripes'` shades every other band. Each ring is labelled with its distance or age along the middle of the radial opening where the gap fits the text, just outside its ring; with no opening the corner scale bar is the only label. Only the arcs crossing the viewport are drawn, so a frame costs one arc per ring in view. New theme tokens `ringWidth` (1), `ringOpacity` (0.3) and `ringStripeOpacity` (0.08), `--phylo-ring-*`; the colour is `muted` and the labels use `axisFontSize`. The playground has the option and the tokens. The scale step of the bar and the rings is one function, `radialStep`, in `draw.ts`.
- **Search says how many tips match, and highlighting is not capped** (#58). **Breaking:** `Search.find(viewer, query)` returns `{ ranks, total }` (new type `SearchResult`) instead of the ranks alone: the tips it would list (at most `maxResults`) and how many match in all, so every label is now scanned (about 40 to 70 ms on the 2.39M labels of OTL, headless). When more tips match than it lists, the `Search` list ends with a line '8 of 1,234 matches' (`.phylo-search__more`, not an option), and a new polite live region (`search.status`) announces '8 of 1,234 matches', '3 matches' or 'No matches'. Highlight bands cost canvas calls per pixel, not per match: rectangular bands that overlap are merged into one `fillRect` per run, only the matches on rows in view are visited, a ring within a pixel of the one before is skipped and the rings are stroked as one path, a radial band within a pixel of the one before (and no wider) is skipped; the overview marks every match (it marked the first 2,000 ranks only) at most once per pixel; `setHighlighted` sorts the ranks as a typed array. On the 2.39M-tip OTL tree, 1M matches in band style took about 9 s per frame at the fit view and now take about 30 ms (radial about 18 s, now about 70 ms); measurements in `docs/design/highlight-cost.md` (`tests/browser/bench/highlight-bench.mjs`). **Behaviour change:** overlapping bands no longer darken where matches are dense. The explorer's search highlights and counts every match (the 5,000 cap and its note are gone, the count is `1 / 12,345`), and the playground's "Highlight tips containing" and the node menu's "Highlight its tips" pass every match (the 20,000 caps are gone); the playground keeps the matches of the same text and tree, as every setting change re-applies them. The explorer and playground browser checks assert it on queries matching more than the old caps, and the playground check the Search count.
- **The level of detail collapses a clade by its height only** (#87). A clade was also collapsed when it was narrower than `minCladePx` and less than four times that tall, so a clade of near-identical tips (subspecies, or sister taxa with tiny branch lengths) stayed a summary at zooms where its tips had rows to spare: with the explorer's `minCladePx` 14 on big trees, a cherry at rows under 28 px, or a single tip under a node of its own at rows under 56 px. Now only the rows count (radial: the arc at the tips): a clade is collapsed when it is less than `minCladePx` tall, however short its branches. The cut goldens are captured again: the cases with clades between `minPx` and `4 * minPx` tall draw their tips instead of a summary, and under budget pressure the threshold climbs higher before the list fits, so a search may now take up to 100 passes instead of 60 (a cold radial frame zoomed 64 times into a 20k comb needed 63). Draw lists of GTDB 190k and OTL 100k in the explorer, rectangular layout, at fit and zoomed 8, 64 and 512 times are unchanged at `minCladePx` 3 and 14.
- **Undo within a memory budget** (#44). New tree-ops `treeBytes(tree)`: an estimate of the memory a tree holds (its typed arrays and 8 bytes per element of its plain arrays; the label strings, shared between an operation's input and result, are not counted), constant time. Measured: one more `TreeModel` keeps 66 to 68 bytes per node in Node on trees of 10k to 1.5M tips, the tree as parsed 128 to 143 with its labels; `treeBytes` gives 74 to 78 (`npm run bench:bytes` in `packages/tree-ops`, figures in `docs/design/undo-memory.md`; the 2.39M OTL tree could not be fetched here and is extrapolated). `examples/explorer` and `examples/react` keep the trees between the one as published and the current one within 64 MiB, dropping the oldest first; Undo stops at the oldest kept, and a tree larger than the budget has Reset only, with a note. The explorer gets a Reset button. `?undo-budget=<bytes>` sets the budget on both pages, and their browser checks use it.
- **React hooks for the viewer, its viewport, theme and events** (#31). `@gbif-phylo/react` exports `useTreeViewer(ref)` (the `TreeViewer` behind a `<TreeView>` as state: `null` until mounted, a new value when the viewer is re-created), `useViewport(viewer)` and `useTreeTheme(viewer)` (the window and the theme in force, through `useSyncExternalStore` on `viewchange` and `themechange`) and `useTreeEvent(viewer, type, handler, opts?)` (any viewer event for the viewer's lifetime; the newest handler is called). They use only the viewer's public API; the core is unchanged. Strict-mode safe, with unit tests rendering them in `StrictMode` (`packages/react/test/hooks.test.mjs`); `check:packages` has a hooks-only scenario. The explorer uses them: its Width / Height sliders are read from the viewport against the fitted window, so they follow a wheel zoom (checked in `check-explorer.mjs`), Fit tree really fits (it used to re-apply the sliders' scales), and the alignment legend reads the theme from `useTreeTheme` instead of a hand-made `themechange` listener. **Behaviour change in the explorer:** a layout, tree type or pane-size change no longer re-applies the slider scales to the new fit; the sliders show what the viewer did.
- **One look for every summary: a faint background with stripes over it** (#85). Striped summaries (#59) were stripes only, drawn like branches, so next to resolved tips they read as branches starting in mid-air. Now every summary (the level of detail's triangles, fans and bands, and the clades an app collapsed, which keep their label) is one background fill of its shape in `summary` at `summaryOpacity` with 1 px stripes in the same colour at the new token `summaryStripeOpacity` over it; a summary without room for two stripes (or whose stripes would be more than eight gaps apart) gets their mean tone (`summaryStripeOpacity` over the distance they would have) as a second fill instead. The same in rectangular, radial and the overview strip, which used to stay solid; a clade colour tints both. **Breaking:** the options `summaryStyle` and `stripeCollapsed` are removed, and so is the theme token `summaryStripeColour` (`--phylo-summary-stripe-colour`; the stripes use `summary` or the clade colour). New token `summaryStripeOpacity` (0.5, `--phylo-summary-stripe-opacity`); `summaryOpacity` now means the background's opacity and its default goes from 0.6 to 0.25; `summaryStripeGap` goes from 3 to 2 px. The stripe width no longer scales with the spacing to match a solid fill: stripes are hairlines and the background carries the shape. The playground drops the style select and the stripe colour and gets a stripe opacity slider, and its dark preset no longer sets its own summary opacity; the explorer drops its Stripes toggle and keeps `minCladePx` 14 on trees over 1,500 tips. `stripes-bench.mjs` measures the one look.
- **The synthetic trees in the examples carry support** (#55). The playground's and the explorer's synthetic entries point at support files, so node labels, collapse by support and the support scale have something to show; the main files stay bare, so the cut goldens, `check-radial-cut.mjs` and the benchmarks read the same trees as before. New committed files in `data/synthetic/newick`: `yule_10000_support.nwk.gz`, `caterpillar_10000_support.nwk.gz`, `polytomy_mixed_22800_support.nwk.gz` (from `make_polytomy_mixed.py`; the 6,900 pairs under the polytomy have uniform support, so most dissolve into it at 0.7) and `yule_1000_bootstrap.nwk` (support 0 to 100), with sizes and hashes in `manifest.json`, which now also lists the two polytomy trees. `check.py` checks each support file's scale and its share below 0.7, and `make check` passes again (it stopped at the polytomy files). Catalogue: `syn-caterpillar-20000` (the hand-made `rich/edge-cases` file) is replaced by `syn-caterpillar-10000`, and `syn-yule-1000-bootstrap` is new. The playground and explorer checks load every synthetic entry, find drawn support values and check that collapse by support at 0.7 changes the tree.
- **A partial source's node ids are bounded by `nodeCount` too** (#79). The viewer checks an id given to `setClades`, `setCollapsed`, `zoomToNode` and the like against `TreeSource.nodeCount` (documented as the node count of the full tree) for a partial source as well, so an id past it warns instead of being accepted; before, only its form was checked. A partial source must report a `nodeCount` above its largest id. The test fixtures that shifted their ids (`wrapped()` in `check-radial-ui.mjs`, the sources in `node-ids` and `node-labels` tests) reported the base tree's count and now report the shifted one.
- **Full detail while moving, unless frames get slow** (#75). `interactionLOD` takes `'auto'`, the new default: frames drawn while the view moves (wheel, pinch, drag, animation) keep full quality, and are reduced (a quarter of the budget, a coarser collapse) only when the full-quality frames are slow, their JS time averaged over the last few above 12 ms. `true` is the old behaviour (always reduced while moving), `false` never reduces. **Default behaviour changes:** the explorer, the playground and every app that did not set the option now keep full detail while panning and zooming a tree that draws fast. Playground: "Reduced frames while moving" is a select (When frames are slow, Always, Never).
- **Length labels on tip branches too** (#66). `nodeLabels: 'length'` now labels the branch of each drawn tip, by the same rule as internal branches (#53): centred on the visible part of the branch, above the line (radial: along it), and only when the text plus the padding fits. On a phylogram the tip branches carry most of the length, and on the HCV tree in the explorer most branches are tip branches, of which none was labelled. Tips are labelled only when the tip rows are far enough apart for tip labels (`labelDensity`), and after all the internal nodes, so the cap (600, radial 300) goes to the internal branches first. With aligned labels the length sits on the tip's own branch, not on the leader. Support and a `nodeLabels` function stay on internal nodes. **Behaviour change:** a tip that stands for a clade collapsed with tree-ops `collapseClades({ extend })` now shows its lengthened branch's length. There is no option to turn tip lengths off.
- **`TreeModel` names nodes by number only** (#78, the io half of #30). **Breaking:** `nodeId`, `nodeIndex`, `isNodeId` and `tipNodeId` are removed (the `'n<id>'` string form is gone; `tipIndex` returns the number). `tipCountOf`, `tipRange`, `descendantTips`, `tipsOf`, `contains`, `children`, `mrca`, `ancestors` and `mrcaOf` take node numbers, not `'n<id>'` strings, and `mrcaOf` no longer takes tip labels: turn a label into a number with `tipIndex` (or `tipIndices`) first, e.g. `tree.mrcaOf(labels.flatMap((l) => tree.tipIndices(l)))`.
- **`setTree`'s kept view follows repeated labels** (#80). With `view: 'keep'` and no `map`, tips are matched by label, and every tip of a repeated label went to the first tip with it in the new tree, so the window could frame the wrong place. The k-th tip with a label in the old tree now goes to the k-th in the new one (`tipIndices`); a tip with no k-th counterpart is left out. Sources without `tipIndices` still match the first tip.
- **Explorer: one ladderize group** (#73). Ascending, descending and the "by" select (tips, depth, height) are one segmented `.ex-group` instead of two controls, and the select has no icon of its own. The buttons use the bars icon that sat on the select (`IconLadderBy`): ascending has the short bar at the top, matching "smallest clades at top", and descending is the same icon mirrored vertically. The old `IconLadderize` is removed. `check-explorer.mjs` checks the group and that ascending puts a smaller top-level clade first than descending.
- **Explorer: one colour for support and length labels, and a plain bootstrap value** (#70). The inspector's Bootstrap value was red; it now has the colour, font and weight of the other values. `--phylo-support-colour` and `--phylo-length-colour` both point at one new variable, `--ex-node-label` (cyan, `#0891b2` light and `#22d3ee` dark, the length labels' colour until now), so support labels are cyan by default and a theme sets one variable to change both; the two tokens stay separate. The Bootstrap toggle in the toolbar follows it. The red `--ex-boot` is renamed `--ex-danger`, as only errors (the page error, the search count with no match, the clear button) still use it. The library's own defaults for `supportColour` and `lengthColour` were not changed (both `''`, meaning `muted`). `check-explorer.mjs` checks both.
- **The radial layout leaves an opening, and can be turned** (#41). New options `radialOpening` (the share of the circle left empty between the last and the first tip, 0 to 0.99, default **0.05**) and `radialRotation` (where the middle of the opening sits, in degrees clockwise from 3 o'clock, default **90**: the bottom). The tips are spread over `(1 - radialOpening) * 360` degrees, each in the middle of its slot, so the first and the last tip no longer sit next to each other; 0.5 is a half circle, an arch open at the bottom. **Default behaviour changes:** the radial layout is 5% open at the bottom, where it used to be a closed circle starting at 3 o'clock; `radialOpening: 0, radialRotation: 0` gives exactly the old angles. Every angle goes through one helper in `layout.ts` (`RadialFan`, `radialFan`, `fanAngle`, `fanTheta`, `fanRow`, `setLayoutFan`; `Layout.fan`, `Transform.fan`), used by the trig cache, the projection and its window, the radial cut (culling and arc lengths), hit-testing (a pointer just inside the opening finds the tip beside it), the fit (it frames the fan's bounding box, so a half circle in a 2:1 container gets twice the scale), the zoom cap, `focusTip`, `zoomToNode`, the keyboard's start node, the view kept by `setTree` and by collapsing, and the overview; tip labels already turned round by their screen angle. **The radial cut goldens were recaptured on purpose** for the new default (`test/cut-golden.json`, 55 of the 70 radial cases changed, the rectangular ones not at all); the same radial cases on the whole circle (`radialFan(0, 0)`) are checked against hashes of the old goldens (`test/cut-golden-circle.json`), and `scripts/check-radial-cut.mjs` takes `--opening` / `--rotation` and runs under `npm test` with openings 0, 0.05 and 0.5. The playground has "Radial opening" and "Radial rotation" sliders, and its browser check hovers and clicks the tips on both sides of the opening.
- **The hover ring grows in** (#47). With the new option `hoverAnimation` (default `true`) the hover ring grows into existence, radius and opacity from 0 with an ease-out, over the new theme token `hoverRingDuration` (150 ms, `--phylo-hover-ring-duration`; 0 = at once); moving to another node fades the old ring out over the same time, and the pointer leaving the tree removes it at once. The keyboard cursor's ring grows the same way; the selection ring and the marks are not animated. Only the overlay canvas is redrawn, one frame per step, and no frame is asked for once the rings have settled. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the rings are drawn at full size at once. The ring's size, a constant until now, is the theme's `hoverRingRadius` (6; 1.5 times it for a collapsed clade) and `hoverRingWidth` (2). The playground gets the toggle and the three tokens; the explorer turns it on.
- **Hover path** (#40). Hovering a tip, clade or collapsed clade also highlights its path to the root, every branch and connector on the way, in both layouts; with nothing hovered the keyboard cursor's path is shown. New option `hoverPath` (default `true`) and theme tokens `hoverPathColour` (`''` = `highlight`) and `hoverPathWidth` (2), with `--phylo-hover-path-*`. It is drawn on the overlay canvas under the rings, so a hover never redraws the tree; on a 100k-tip caterpillar the path of the deepest tip (99,999 edges) takes about 3 ms of CPU and about 1,100 canvas calls. The playground has the toggle and the two tokens.
- **A ring on the node an action is for** (#65). Opening the `NodeMenu` selects its node (a click already did, by default; now `openAt` from an app does too), so the selection ring stays on the node while the pointer is in the menu. New `viewer.setMarked(nodes)` / `getMarked()` and `<TreeView marked>` mark nodes with a dashed ring of their own until cleared, for the pending pick of a two-step action; new theme tokens `markColour` (`''` = `highlight`), `markWidth` (2), `markRadius` (11) and `markDash` (`'3 2'`), with `--phylo-mark-*`. The rings follow the nodes through pans, zooms, layout switches and collapsing, and `setTree` sends them through `map`. The explorer's Swap marks its first pick until the second click or Escape; the playground has the four tokens. Behaviour change: `NodeMenu.openAt` called by an app now selects the node too (and the selection stays after the menu closes).
- **Duplicate tip labels: labels are lookup keys, not identities** (#18). **Breaking:** `TreeModel.tipIndex` now returns the FIRST tip with a label, not the last; `getHighlighted()` and `highlightchange` are typed `TipRef[]` (`string | number`), not `string[]`; the `Search` control with `highlight: true` puts the chosen tip's node id (a number), not its label, into `getHighlighted()` / `highlightchange`. A label on several tips used to name only the last of them (`TreeModel.tipIndex` kept one tip per label), so highlighting, search and column lookups reached one tip and nothing said so. Now: `setHighlighted` (and `<TreeView highlighted>`) take labels and node ids (`TipRef = string | number`): a label highlights every tip with it, a number that tip alone; a number that is not a node or is an internal node is ignored with a `console.warn`; `getHighlighted` and `highlightchange` give back both kinds, and `setTree` sends highlighted node ids through `map`. `tipNode(label)` is the first tip with the label, new `tipNodes(label)` all of them. Column `values` keyed by label apply to every tip with it; new `nodeValues` (a `Map` by node id) for values of one tip, also passed to custom renderers' `prepare` with `tipNodes`. `<TreeView>`'s `focus`, `focusTip`, `locateTip` and `columnCell` take a label or a node id; a label takes its first tip, and `focus` / `focusTip` warn when several tips carry it. The `Search` control lists each matching tip ('2 of 3' for a label on several), focuses and highlights the chosen one by node id, and `onSelect` gets its node id as a third argument. `@gbif-phylo/io`: new `tipIndices(label)` and `duplicateTipLabels()` (the labels on more than one tip, empty for unique labels, so an app can warn after parsing), and `mrcaOf` counts every tip of a label. `TreeData` gets an optional `tipIndices`. tree-ops' `collapseClades` no longer needs unique labels from a `label` function. The explorer's search counts and steps through each matching tip. New test tree `data/rich/edge-cases/duplicate-tip-labels.nwk`, in the catalogue as "Duplicate tip labels".
- **Nodes are numbers everywhere** (#30). **Breaking** for `<TreeView>`: `clades` is `{ node: number, colour, label? }[]` (was `{ nodeId: 'n<id>' }`), `collapsed` is `number[]`, and the handle's `zoomToNode`, `locateNode` and `nodeBounds` take a number. The events (`TreeEvent`, so also `TreeNodeEvent`) lose `nodeId`, the `'n<id>'` string form of `node`: use `node`. A node is the preorder id of the tree the viewer holds, the same number as in tree-ops' `map` and `TreeModel`, so an id from a click goes straight back. Tips can still be named by label (`highlighted`, `focus`, `focusTip`, `locateTip`). An id that is not a node of the tree (a string such as `'n12'`, a fraction, a negative id, or for a tree in memory an id past the last node; a partial source only checks the form) passed to `setClades`, `setCollapsed`, `setTree`'s `clades` / `collapsed`, `zoomToNode`, `locateNode` or `nodeBounds` is still ignored, now with a `console.warn` instead of silently. The rule is in one place in `docs/api.md` ("Naming nodes"). `examples/explorer`, `examples/react`, `examples/vanilla` and the test page drop the `slice(1)` / `'n' +` conversions.
- **Striped summaries** (#59, second part). New option `summaryStyle: 'solid' | 'striped'` (default `'solid'`, nothing changes) fills level-of-detail summaries (triangles, fans, polytomy and run bands) with fine stripes in the direction the hidden branches would run: rectangular, horizontal, about one per hidden row and at least `summaryStripeGap` px apart; radial, along the rays from the root, spaced by angle. New theme tokens `summaryStripeColour` (`''` = `summary`) and `summaryStripeGap` (default 3 px), with `--phylo-summary-stripe-colour` and `--phylo-summary-stripe-gap`. Clades collapsed by the app (`setCollapsed`, #29) stay solid unless the new option `stripeCollapsed` is true. Dimming (#22) applies as to the solid fill; the overview strip stays solid; a summary with room for fewer than two stripes stays solid. The stripes' width and alpha are set so their mean darkness is the solid fill's (width `max(1, a * pitch)`, alpha `a * pitch / width`, `a` the solid alpha, dimmed too; within 12% measured at pitches 3 to 20 px). `summaryStripeGap` below 1 is taken as 1. Radial rays form one grid over the fan's whole extent and only those at visible angles are drawn (every 2nd, 4th, ... past 256), so panning does not shimmer. Summaries whose stripes would be more than eight gaps apart stay solid. The overview strip stays solid by design. Segments are collected into one reused buffer and stroked in batches (no allocation per node; rectangular generates only the visible rows, radial at most 256 rays per fan). - **`reroot` and `midpointRoot` keep support that exists only in `tree.support`** (#48). - **tree-ops `ladderise(t, { by })`** (#49). `by: 'tips' | 'depth' | 'height'` (default `'tips'`, output unchanged). `'depth'` is the most nested path from a child to a tip in nodes, `'height'` the longest path below the child in branch length (the child's own edge is not counted: crown height or age, so sibling ties on dated trees stay ties; missing or negative lengths count as 0; heights within 1e-6 of the greatest are equal; without lengths every edge counts 1, so it equals depth). Ties go to the other measures in a fixed order (depth: tips, height; height: tips, depth), then the input order (`order: 'decreasing'` reverses the tie-break measures too); `'tips'` keeps going straight to the input order so existing results do not change. Unknown `by` or `order` values throw a RangeError. One reverse-preorder pass computes the measures in use. The playground does not expose ladderise, so there is no UI.
- **`viewer.setTree(tree | source, { map?, view?, clades?, highlighted?, collapsed? })`** (#27). A new tree no longer re-creates the renderer.
